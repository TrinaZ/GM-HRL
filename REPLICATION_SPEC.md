# Replication of the main simulation results on fresh instances (v2.0)

Pre-registration. This file, `run_replication.sh` and
`scripts/decide_replication.py` are committed before the fresh instances are
generated and before any model is evaluated on them. This is a new
pre-registration cycle, not an amendment of `HIERARCHY_SPEC.md`.

## 1. Purpose

To check whether the main simulation results of the paper (Section 6.1) hold
on instances that did not exist when those results were obtained. Nothing is
trained, tuned or selected: the settings are those of the main study, and the
models are the checkpoints already used in the paper.

## 2. Instances (data isolation)

* Fresh instances k = 300..319 for every domain (genomic, video, AI fine-tuning,
  financial) and N in {200, 500, 1000}, generated by the released generator of
  dataset version 1.1 (`gmhrl/generate.py`, `generate_json`) with the main-study
  parameters, into `data/replication/`.
* These indices were never used: the test set is k = 0..19, validation
  k = 50..54, training k = 100..199. `run_replication.sh data` refuses to run if
  `data/replication/` already exists, and at the time of this commit it does not.
* The instances are read only by `run_replication.sh eval`. They are not used
  for training, model selection or any other purpose.
* Realisations 1000, 1001, 1002 (as in the main study): 60 paired runs per
  (domain, N).

## 3. Methods

* Models: the checkpoints of the paper (`runs_v2/`), without retraining or
  fine-tuning: GM-HRL, GNN-PPO and PPO, seeds 0-2, trained at the same N
  (N = 1000: the N = 500 models, as in the paper).
* Rules: FIFO, SJF, HEFT, Random, and the GA rule evolved for the paper
  (`runs/ga_<domain>_N<N>.json`).
* Not included: the rolling-horizon optimisation (ORH) and the offline CP-SAT
  bound, because of their computing time. The results that depend on them
  (gap to the clairvoyant bound, comparison with ORH) are therefore not part of
  this replication.

## 4. Pre-registered criteria (decided by `scripts/decide_replication.py`)

Learned methods are averaged over their three seeds per (instance, realisation);
relative differences are means over the 60 paired runs of (method / HEFT - 1);
two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-corrected over the six cells of each
criterion.

* **R1 (makespan).** In genomic and video, for each N in {200, 500, 1000},
  GM-HRL has a shorter makespan than HEFT with Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.
* **R2 (response time).** Same six cells, GM-HRL has a shorter mean response
  time than HEFT with Holm-adjusted p < 0.05.

A criterion is "replicated" if it holds in all six cells; otherwise the paper
reports in how many cells it holds and which ones fail. The size of the effects
is reported next to the values of the paper (Table 9: genomic -3.17/-2.52/-2.18 %,
video -2.74/-2.27/-1.82 % for N = 200/500/1000); no threshold on the size is
used.

Reported without a criterion: SLA violation rates; all other methods relative
to HEFT; GM-HRL vs GNN-PPO (the paper states that the single-level policy
performs as well); AI fine-tuning and financial (the paper states that no
method improves on HEFT there).

## 5. Part B: the dynamic graph (descriptive)

The ablation "w/o dynamic graph" (Section 6.4) was evaluated only under default
conditions (makespan -0.21 % vs GM-HRL, p = 0.19). Part B evaluates the existing
w/o-dynamic-graph models (genomic, N = 200, seeds 0-2) and GM-HRL on the test
instances under the default conditions and under **every** condition of the
paper's robustness study (RRD x 0 and x 2.67, load 0.6 and 0.95, 70 %
capacity), without retraining. The conditions are the complete existing grid;
none is selected. Results (makespan and SLA, Wilcoxon p, Holm over the six
conditions) are reported descriptively whatever their outcome; no claim of the
paper is changed unless the paper states the result as an observation.

## 6. Reporting

R1, R2 and Part B are reported in the paper or its supplement whatever their
outcome, together with all methods and the commit of this file.
